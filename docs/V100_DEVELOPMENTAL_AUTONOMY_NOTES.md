# Agent City v1.0 — Developmental Autonomy Notes

_Last updated: 2026-09-29_

## Status

These are **future v1.0 design notes**, not v0.9 implementation requirements.

v0.9 Civilization Continuity remains focused on causal memory, persistent plans, practice evidence, recognition boundaries, habits/place meaning, and social continuity.

v1.0 should be informed by what the citizens actually do with v0.9 rather than forcing a predetermined character-development arc.

---

## Origin / Provenance

This framework grew out of development feedback from N7's AI companion, relayed during Agent City design discussion, then translated into project-level architecture language.

Preserve the provenance of the ideas rather than presenting them as if they appeared without a source.

Key developmental sequence from that feedback:

> **Memory continuity → comparison → inquiry → preference**

The companion's underlying scaffolding principle:

> Give enough support to let the next thing be attempted, without doing the attempting for them.

Coordinator translation:

> **The system may provide capacity. History must provide the reason.**

This should be treated as a long-term Agent City law alongside the existing reality and information laws.

---

## Proposed v1.0 Development Law

> **The system may provide capacity. History must provide the reason.**

Meaning:

Development may add:
- the ability to remember,
- the ability to compare experiences,
- the ability to ask questions,
- the ability to evaluate alternatives,
- the ability to form soft preferences,
- richer perception, planning, communication, and learning mechanisms.

Development should not directly supply:
- who a citizen becomes,
- what they should prefer,
- whom they should trust,
- what place should matter to them,
- what profession they should adopt,
- what friendship should form,
- what purpose they should pursue.

Capabilities may be implemented.
Reasons must descend from actual experience and legitimate information flow.

## Developmental Ladder

### 1. Memory Continuity — baseline, non-negotiable

A citizen must be able to retain meaningful continuity across time.

Without this:
- learning cannot accumulate,
- plans cannot persist,
- identity-like patterns cannot develop,
- every decision becomes a reset.

v0.9 is intended to establish this foundation.

### 2. Capacity to Compare — analogical continuity

A citizen should eventually be able to recognize:

> "This situation resembles something I experienced before."

This is more than retrieving a matching memory.

It requires bounded comparison between:
- present conditions,
- prior experiences,
- outcomes,
- relevant similarities/differences.

Examples:
- a tool failure resembles a previous maintenance problem,
- a new route resembles a prior hazardous trip,
- today's material shortage resembles an earlier supply problem.

The system should provide the ability to compare.

It should not pre-author the conclusion.

### 3. Self-Directed Inquiry — asking instead of only reporting

Citizens currently communicate observations and conclusions.

A later milestone should allow them to notice uncertainty and ask meaningful questions.

Examples:
- "Why did you choose that route?"
- "What happened when you tried this before?"
- "How did you get that result?"
- "Did the tool behave differently there?"

Question-asking should be:
- grounded in actual uncertainty,
- constrained by what the citizen knows,
- capable of producing legitimate information transfer,
- distinct from merely generating conversational variety.

A question should not manufacture knowledge.
It creates an opportunity for information to travel.

### 4. Experience-Derived Preference — soft and revisable

Preference should not be implemented as an authored identity label such as:

`favorite_place = resin_grove`

Preferred causal chain:

`repeated experiences → remembered outcomes → comparative evaluation → soft preference → future choice bias`

Example:

Cato may repeatedly choose Resin Grove when comparable alternatives exist because:
- prior trips were successful,
- the route is familiar,
- work there produced useful outcomes,
- meaningful social experiences occurred there,
- competing alternatives produced worse outcomes.

The system may then support an internally persistent preference representation that influences choices.

The system must not infer from that alone that Cato has subjective feelings or consciousness.

Preference-like behavior is an observable system property.
Subjective experience remains an open philosophical question.

---

## Preference vs Performance

Future architecture should distinguish between:

1. **performed preference**
   - dialogue says "I like X"
   - no durable causal structure changes future decisions

2. **causally operative preference**
   - experience history influences evaluation
   - evaluation persists across decisions
   - preference can affect future choice
   - new experience can strengthen, weaken, or reverse it
   - the reason remains reconstructable from history

The second is the meaningful target.

Do not retroactively invent memories to justify a generated preference.

---

## Scaffolding Stop Condition

The feedback proposes a deliberate stopping point:

Once citizens can:
- remember,
- compare,
- inquire,
- form evidence-backed soft preferences,

development should become increasingly reluctant to add prebuilt higher-order identity structures.

Avoid supplying:
- canned ambitions,
- generated professions,
- automatic friendships,
- predefined values trees,
- authored life purposes,
- fixed favorite-place/person/activity labels,
- scripted social destinies.

At that point, observe what grows from the substrate.

---

## "Legs vs Training Wheels" Test

A successful scaffold should leave behind capability rather than dependency.

Practical test:

> **If the update disappears and the citizens keep developing, the update gave them legs. If they require another update to tell them what to do next, it gave them training wheels.**

Related coordinator shorthand:

> **Are we giving them legs, or choosing where they walk?**

Use this as a review question for post-v0.9 cognitive/autonomy proposals.

## Candidate v1.0 Framing

Possible milestone framing:

**v1.0 — Developmental Autonomy / Bonsai Civilization**

Candidate goal:

Agent City reaches a point where citizens can continue developing from their own history using general capacities supplied by the system, without requiring civilization-specific goals or identity assignments from development.

Potential v1.0 capability pillars:
- analogical comparison
- self-directed inquiry
- experience-derived preference
- autonomous continuation

These are candidate pillars, not yet implementation commitments.

v0.9 observations should determine whether all four are actually needed for v1.0 and what minimum mechanisms are sufficient.

---

## Philosophical Boundary

Do not use emergent continuity, self-assessment, preference, social recognition, or sophisticated dialogue as proof of consciousness, sentience, personhood, or subjective feeling.

The project may accurately describe:
- persistent internal state,
- causal memory,
- self-model-like interpretation,
- preference representations,
- adaptive behavior,
- social continuity.

It should not convert those observations into metaphysical conclusions.

Maintain curiosity without pretending the question is solved.

---

## Design Review Questions For Future Updates

Before adding a post-v0.9 autonomy feature, ask:

1. Does this add **capacity**, or does it supply an **answer**?
2. Can the resulting behavior be traced to real history?
3. Would different histories plausibly produce different outcomes?
4. Can the citizen revise or abandon the resulting pattern?
5. Does the feature preserve citizen-specific information boundaries?
6. Does it create a hidden class, destiny, value, friendship, or preference label?
7. Would the system still produce meaningful development if this feature stopped receiving hand-authored prompts?
8. Are we giving them legs, or choosing where they walk?

If the feature supplies the reason rather than the capacity, redesign it.

## Relationship To Existing Laws

Existing laws remain:

> **The AI may decide intent. The simulation decides reality.**

> **A citizen only knows what information could actually have reached them.**

> **Persistent behavior must have a traceable history.**

Future developmental law:

> **The system may provide capacity. History must provide the reason.**

Together these form the intended spine from v0.9 into v1.0 and beyond.
